How to Quote Progressive

A first-pass Progressive quote that's wrong on price is worse than no quote — the prospect assumes we're careless, or worse, that we're cheap because we're hiding something. This guide is the checklist for getting it right on the first try, the discounts to ask about every time, the quirks that catch new agents, and how to present the number so it actually closes.

📌 Golden rule: a quote is only as good as the intake. Every field you guess or skip becomes a stipulation, a rate jump at binding, or a lost sale when the "final" price doesn't match what you said on the phone. Slow down on intake, move fast on everything after.

TL;DR — the 10-second version

  1. Run it through our comparative rater first — it already pulls Progressive in real time. Only log into Progressive for Agents directly for edge cases the rater can't touch (SR-22, named non-owner, some commercial).
  2. Get the full driver's license number and DOB for every household resident, not just the ones being insured — Progressive rates and sometimes requires excluding the rest.
  3. Get the VIN for every vehicle. A year/make/model guess is close enough to be wrong.
  4. Ask for the current bodily-injury liability limits and confirm no lapse in coverage — both move price more than almost anything else you'll enter.
  5. Always quote auto + home together before you quote either alone — the bundle discount is the single biggest lever you control.
  6. Offer Snapshot on every auto quote. Even a "no thanks" now plants it for the renewal call.
  7. Present it side by side against what they're paying today — never as a number floating on its own.

1 · Where the quote actually happens

  • Comparative rater first. Run the household through our rater (the same tool behind the /quote renewal-comparison page) — it already returns a real-time Progressive premium alongside the other carriers we write, from one intake.
  • Progressive for Agents (ForAgents.com) direct is for the cases the rater can't handle cleanly: SR-22/FR-44 filings, a named non-owner policy, a vehicle that needs manual VIN decoding, or anything the rater comes back "refer" on. Log in with your own agent credentials — never share logins across desks.
  • Whichever path you use, the underlying answers are the same — the checklist below applies either way.

3 · Getting the price right the first time

  • Use the real VIN. Trim level, safety features, and even the engine can change the rate for the "same" model year. If the customer doesn't have it handy, the registration or insurance card has it — don't proceed on a guess if you can help it.
  • Don't round the liability limits. "Probably 25/50" and "actually 100/300" are a real premium gap. If they don't know, ask them to check their current ID card or declarations page before you finalize.
  • Confirm the lapse question honestly. A gap in coverage — even a short one while switching carriers — triggers a real surcharge. It's better to flag it up front than have it appear as a rate jump at binding.
  • Get the garaging address right if it's different from the mailing address (a kid away at school with the car, a second home). Progressive rates by where the car actually lives.
  • List every household resident, even ones not being insured on this policy. Unlisted or unrated household drivers are the most common reason a bound policy gets re-rated or non-renewed later.

5 · Quirks that catch new agents

  • Household exclusions. A resident with a bad driving record doesn't have to be insured — but they usually have to be formally excluded on the policy, in writing, or their record still touches the rate (and their driving the car becomes uninsured).
  • Named non-owner policies (liability-only, for someone who drives but doesn't own a car) aren't handled by the standard auto quote flow — these go through Progressive for Agents directly.
  • Home isn't always written by Progressive itself. Depending on the state, the homeowners policy in a Progressive bundle may be underwritten by a partner carrier on Progressive's paper. Set that expectation with the customer up front so the declarations page and insurer name on the bill don't surprise them.
  • Umbrella eligibility generally requires both the underlying auto and home (or renters) to be with Progressive at adequate limits — don't offer it as a bolt-on until the bundle is actually in place.
  • High-risk / SR-22 business routes differently than a standard quote — flag it early rather than discovering it after you've built out a full standard quote.

6 · When the rater comes back "refer" or "no-hit"

  • Don't re-run it hoping for a different answer. A refer usually means a manual underwriting step (prior total-loss claim, a lapse over the threshold, a home with an aging roof) — that's a call to Progressive for Agents or the underwriting desk, not a retry with slightly different numbers.
  • A "no-hit" on driving history (new driver, recently moved from another country, no U.S. credit/insurance history) is normal — don't treat it as an error. Note it and continue; it affects rating, not eligibility.
  • Roof age or condition on a home referral — ask if a roof has been replaced or inspected; get the year if so. It's often the one fact standing between a quote and a referral.

7 · Presenting the quote to close

  • Never send a number alone. Put it next to what they're paying today — same coverage limits side by side, not just the bottom-line premium. This is the whole point of comparing against their renewal, not against a generic average.
  • Lead with the coverage match, not just the price. "Same liability limits you have now, same deductible, and here's the difference" builds more trust than a bare dollar figure.
  • Name the discounts you applied out loud — "that number already includes the bundle and paperless" — so the price doesn't feel arbitrary.
  • Create urgency honestly. If their current policy renews soon, that's a real deadline, not manufactured pressure: "this rate is locked in for the next six months if we move before your renewal."
  • If they hesitate, ask what's stopping them rather than re-quoting silently — it's usually one unanswered question (claims history on file, timing of the switch, the current company's cancellation fee), not the price itself.
