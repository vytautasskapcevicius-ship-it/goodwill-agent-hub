Landlord Insurance in Elmhurst, IL & Across Illinois

Renting out a house, condo, or 2–4 unit building? A landlord policy (DP-3) protects the building, your rental income, and you personally when a tenant or guest gets hurt — things your homeowners policy stops covering the day a tenant moves in.

What landlord insurance covers

Landlord insurance — most often written as a DP-3 (Dwelling Fire) policy — is built for property you own but don't live in. A good Illinois landlord policy typically includes:

  • Dwelling coverage — rebuilds or repairs the structure after fire, wind, hail, vandalism, and other covered causes
  • Other structures — detached garages, fences, sheds on the rental property
  • Loss of rental income — keeps rent checks coming while the unit is uninhabitable after a covered loss
  • Landlord liability — legal defense and judgments if a tenant or visitor is injured on the property
  • Landlord's own property — appliances, tools, and maintenance equipment you keep on site (not the tenant's belongings)

Optional add-ons worth discussing: water backup, building-code/ordinance upgrades on older Chicagoland housing stock, vandalism & malicious mischief for vacancies, and rent-guarantee style endorsements where available.

Landlord insurance vs. homeowners insurance

This is the question that catches most new landlords. A homeowners (HO-3) policy assumes you live in the home. Once the property becomes a rental, many carriers can deny claims that happen under a tenancy — because the risk was never underwritten that way. The fix is simple: the policy follows the use of the property.

  • You live there → homeowners policy
  • A tenant lives there → landlord (DP-3) policy
  • You live in one unit, rent the others (owner-occupied 2–4 flat) → a homeowners or dwelling policy set up specifically for owner-occupied rentals — tell us, this is common in Chicagoland
  • Renting your old home "temporarily" → still a landlord policy — "accidental landlords" are the most common uncovered-claim story we see

Cost-wise, expect a landlord policy to run roughly 15–25% more than a comparable homeowners policy, because tenant-occupied property claims more often. Losing a $30,000 claim to the wrong policy type costs far more than that difference.

Renting on Airbnb or short-term?

Short-term and vacation rentals are the fastest-growing corner of landlord insurance — and the most misunderstood. Most standard landlord policies exclude short-term-rental activity, and Airbnb's host guarantee is not an insurance policy you control. If you host in Illinois — whether a whole home, an in-law unit, or seasonal weeks at a second property — ask us about short-term-rental endorsements and dedicated STR programs. Own several rentals? We can look at a multi-property or landlord portfolio setup so every door is on one schedule with one renewal date.

What landlord insurance does not cover

Your policy covers the building and your liability — not your tenant's belongings. If a kitchen fire ruins a tenant's furniture and electronics, that's their loss to insure. That's why experienced landlords require renters insurance in the lease: it protects the tenant, and it puts a liability policy (and its lawyers) between you and many tenant-caused claims. It costs your tenant roughly the price of a pizza a month — see our renters insurance page for what it covers and why leases require it.

Also outside the policy: normal wear and tear, intentional tenant damage on some forms (ask — carriers differ), flood (separate policy — we write those), and anything commercial-scale: buildings with 5+ units belong on a commercial property policy, which we also place.

One lawsuit shouldn't reach your other properties

Liability is where landlords get hurt worst. An icy stair, a deck rail, a dog bite at your rental — an injury judgment can pass right through the policy limit and reach your savings, your other properties, and future income. For a few hundred dollars a year, an umbrella policy adds $1–5 million of liability protection across your rentals, home, and autos. If you own even one rental plus your own home, the umbrella conversation is worth ten minutes.

Frequently asked questions

How much is landlord insurance in Illinois?

It depends on the property's location, age, construction, coverage limits, and claims history — as a rule of thumb, plan on roughly 15–25% more than an equivalent homeowners policy on the same building. We quote it across multiple carriers, so you see the real market for your specific property instead of one company's number.

Is landlord insurance required in Illinois?

No Illinois law requires it — but if the property has a mortgage, your lender almost certainly does. More practically: without it, a fire or an injury lawsuit at a tenant-occupied property can land entirely on you, because a homeowners policy may not respond once the home is rented.

What is a DP-3 policy?

DP-3 (Dwelling Fire, Special Form) is the most common policy form for rental houses and small residential rentals. It covers the dwelling on an open-perils basis (everything except listed exclusions), plus loss of rental income and landlord liability. DP-1 and DP-2 are narrower, named-perils versions — cheaper, but they cover less.

Does landlord insurance cover tenant damage?

Accidental damage from covered causes (like a kitchen fire a tenant starts) is generally covered. Gradual damage, neglect, and normal wear are not, and intentional tenant damage varies by carrier and form. A security deposit, good screening, and requiring renters insurance in the lease close most of that gap.

Does it cover my tenant's belongings?

No. Your policy covers the building and your liability. The tenant's furniture, electronics, and clothing are covered only by their own renters insurance — which is why most leases require it.

I'm renting out a condo I own — what do I need?

A condo-landlord policy (often an HO-6 set up for tenant occupancy, or a dwelling form) covering the interior finishes, your liability, and loss of rent — coordinated with what the association's master policy already covers. Bring your association documents to the quote and we'll line the two up.

What about Airbnb or short-term rentals?

Standard landlord policies usually exclude short-term-rental activity, and platform "host guarantees" are not a substitute for your own coverage. There are dedicated STR endorsements and programs — tell us how you host and we'll match the right one.
