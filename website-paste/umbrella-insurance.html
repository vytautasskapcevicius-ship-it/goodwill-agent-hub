Umbrella Insurance in Elmhurst, IL

An umbrella policy adds extra liability protection on top of your auto, home, and other policies — so one bad accident or lawsuit doesn't reach your savings, home equity, or future income.

What umbrella insurance actually does

Umbrella insurance is extra liability coverage that sits on top of the liability limits already in your auto, homeowners, renters, motorcycle, and watercraft policies. When a covered claim runs past those underlying limits, the umbrella picks up where they stop — usually in $1 million layers, often up to $5 million or more.

Here is the plain-English version. Say your auto policy covers $250,000 in bodily injury and you cause an accident that seriously injures someone. The medical bills, lost wages, and a court judgment come to $700,000. Your auto policy pays its $250,000, and you are personally on the hook for the other $450,000 — your savings, your home equity, even future paychecks can be pursued. A $1 million umbrella would cover that gap.

It is one of the least expensive ways to protect a family. For most Chicagoland households, $1 million of umbrella coverage runs a few hundred dollars a year — far less than the exposure it removes.

  • Adds liability over your auto, home, renters, motorcycle, and boat policies
  • Typically sold in $1M increments, commonly up to $5M
  • Covers large injury claims, property damage you cause, and some lawsuits
  • Can include certain personal injury claims like libel or slander that home and auto policies exclude

Who needs umbrella insurance

Most people who should carry umbrella insurance do not — usually because no one ever offered it to them. The honest test is simple: if a serious lawsuit could reach more than your current auto or home liability limits, you have a gap. The more you own and the more risk in your daily life, the bigger that gap.

We see this often with Elmhurst and Chicagoland families who have paid down a home, added a teen driver, or built up retirement savings. Those are exactly the assets a judgment can target, and they are the reason the conversation is worth ten minutes.

  • You own a home with equity, or have meaningful savings or investments
  • You have a teen or young adult driver in the household
  • You have a swimming pool, trampoline, or a dog
  • You host gatherings, coach, or sit on a nonprofit board
  • You own a rental property or a second home
  • Your net worth is higher than the liability limits on your current policies

How much coverage and what it costs

A common starting point is $1 million, with families who own more often choosing $2 million or higher. A useful rule of thumb is to carry at least as much umbrella coverage as your total net worth, since that is roughly what a serious judgment could put at risk.

Pricing depends on your carrier, the underlying policies, drivers in the household, and how much coverage you choose — so we will not promise a number sight unseen. What we can say is that umbrella is priced as catastrophe coverage, which is why a large limit costs comparatively little. As an independent agency, we compare umbrella quotes across multiple carriers rather than push one company's product.

Most carriers require you to carry minimum underlying liability limits on your auto and home before they will add an umbrella. When you bundle the umbrella with those policies through us, the underlying requirements are easy to line up in one review.

  • Coverage usually starts at $1M and scales up by the million
  • Carriers require minimum underlying auto/home liability limits first
  • We shop multiple carriers — no single-company loyalty tax
  • Right-sizing your limit is part of the conversation, not an upsell

What an umbrella does not cover

An umbrella is liability coverage, so it pays for harm you cause to other people or their property. It does not pay to repair your own car, your own home, or your own belongings — that is what your auto and homeowners policies are for. It also does not cover your business liability; commercial exposure needs a commercial policy.

It will not cover intentional acts, criminal conduct, or claims you took on by contract. And because it builds on your underlying policies, it generally only extends coverage where the auto or home policy beneath it already responds. We will walk through exactly where your umbrella applies so there are no surprises at claim time.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need umbrella insurance if I already have good auto and home coverage?

Possibly. Auto and home liability limits usually top out around $250,000 to $500,000, but a serious injury lawsuit can run well past that. An umbrella adds $1 million or more on top, so a single accident does not reach your savings or home equity. If your net worth is higher than your current liability limits, you have a gap worth closing.

How much does umbrella insurance cost in Illinois?

Cost depends on your carrier, your underlying auto and home policies, the drivers in your household, and the limit you choose, so we quote it case by case. Because it is catastrophe coverage, a large $1 million limit is generally one of the most affordable protections you can add. We compare multiple carriers to find the best fit for your situation.

Does umbrella insurance cover my own car or house?

No. An umbrella is liability coverage — it pays for injury or property damage you cause to others. Damage to your own vehicle or home is covered by your auto and homeowners policies. The umbrella sits on top of those, extending your liability protection when a claim exceeds the underlying limits.

Can I buy an umbrella policy if my auto and home are with another company?

Often yes, though most carriers require you to carry minimum underlying liability limits on those policies first. It is usually simplest to bundle the umbrella with your auto and home through one agency so the underlying limits line up. We can review what you have now and tell you the cleanest path.
